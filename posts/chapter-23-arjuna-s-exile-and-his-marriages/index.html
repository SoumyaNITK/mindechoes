Chapter 23 - Arjuna’s Exile and His Marriages

2025-06-27

One day, something unexpected happened in Indraprastha. Arjuna had to break the rule the Pandavas had made.

Why Arjuna Went to Exile

One of the brothers needed help to protect a Brahmin (a holy man) who was in trouble. Arjuna had to enter the weapons room, which was inside the room where Draupadi and another brother were staying that year.

Even though it was an emergency, Arjuna broke the rule. And he had promised earlier:

“If I break the rule, I will go into exile for 12 years.”

So, like a true hero, Arjuna kept his word. He left Indraprastha and went into the forest to live alone.

Arjuna’s Adventures

While Arjuna was in exile, he visited many places. He met different people and had exciting adventures.

Arjuna Marries Ulupi

One day, Arjuna went to the Ganga river to take a bath. Suddenly, a Naga princess named Ulupi pulled him into the water. She had fallen in love with Arjuna.

Ulupi asked Arjuna to marry her. Arjuna first said no because he was in exile. But she reminded him that his vow was only about Draupadi, not about other marriages. Arjuna agreed and married Ulupi. They had a son named Iravan.

Arjuna Marries Chitrangada

Later, Arjuna reached Manipur, a beautiful kingdom. The king there had a lovely daughter named Chitrangada. Arjuna asked for her hand in marriage.

The king agreed but said:

“My daughter will stay in Manipur always, even after marriage.”

Arjuna agreed, married Chitrangada, and they had a son named Babruvahana. After some time, Arjuna said goodbye to her and continued his journey.

Arjuna Marries Subhadra

Next, Arjuna went to Dwaraka, where Krishna lived. There, he saw Subhadra, Krishna’s sister. She was kind, brave, and beautiful. Arjuna fell in love with her.

Krishna was happy and supported their love. He helped Arjuna and Subhadra secretly leave Dwaraka and get married.

Subhadra later gave birth to a great warrior—Abhimanyu.

Key Takeaway:
Even in exile, Arjuna stayed brave and followed his promises. His marriages brought many new connections and important children who would play big roles later in the Mahabharata.
