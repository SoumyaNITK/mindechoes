Chapter 27 - The Invitation to the Game of Dice

2025-07-01

After the Rajasuya Yagna, Yudhishthira’s fame spread across all of Bharat. Kings from faraway lands came to honor him. But not everyone was happy with his growing power—especially Duryodhana.

Duryodhana had seen the grand palace of Indraprastha and felt humiliated. He was jealous of the Pandavas’ wealth, their crystal-clear halls, and the magical palace built by the great architect Maya. He even slipped and fell into what he thought was solid ground, only to realize it was water. Draupadi laughed and made a sharp comment, “The blind son of a blind king.” This made Duryodhana boil with anger and shame.

He returned to Hastinapura, fuming. His uncle Shakuni, the prince of Gandhara, saw this and smiled. Shakuni was clever and wicked. He knew that war would be risky, but there was another way to defeat the Pandavas—through a game of dice.

Shakuni was a master at playing dice and using loaded ones. He suggested a plan to Duryodhana: “Invite Yudhishthira to play. I’ll play on your behalf. He loves dharma, and he won’t say no to an invitation.”

Duryodhana went to his father, King Dhritarashtra, and requested him to arrange a game between the Kauravas and Pandavas. At first, Dhritarashtra hesitated. But Duryodhana played on his emotions and jealousy. “They are growing more powerful every day,” he said. “If we don’t act now, they will one day take your throne.”

Blinded by love for his son and unsure of what to do, Dhritarashtra gave in. He sent an invitation to Yudhishthira to come to Hastinapura and play dice.

When the messenger arrived in Indraprastha, Yudhishthira was troubled. He knew that gambling was dangerous. But he also believed it was his duty to accept a royal invitation. Saying no would be rude and dishonorable.

So, Yudhishthira agreed.

The Pandavas, along with Draupadi, left for Hastinapura, not knowing that a dangerous trap had been set for them—a game that would change their lives forever.
