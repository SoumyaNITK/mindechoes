Chapter 18 - Kunti’s Mistake and Draupadi’s Five Husbands

2025-06-22

The Pandavas had returned home with Draupadi, their eyes gleaming with triumph. Arjuna called out from outside the house, “Mother! Look what we have brought!”

Kunti, who was inside and unaware of what "they had brought," replied without turning around, “Whatever it is, share it equally among yourselves.”

The moment she turned and saw Draupadi standing beside her sons, she gasped. Her words, though innocent and unaware, had unknowingly set in motion a dilemma — for Draupadi was not a thing to be shared, but a princess. And yet, her words had been uttered before all present.

Yudhishthira, wise and ever calm, pondered over the situation. “A mother’s word is sacred,” he said solemnly, “and we must honor it.”

Draupadi, though initially shocked, gracefully agreed to accept the decision if it pleased Krishna (Lord Vishnu) and Dharma. Soon, the issue reached the ears of the sages and elders. After much debate and deliberation, the sage Vyasa himself arrived.

Vyasa explained that Draupadi was born from the fire for a divine purpose and that in a past life, she had asked for a husband with five different virtues — unknowingly asking for five different men. The boon had been granted, and now, as a result, she would be the wife of five husbands in this life.

With this divine justification, the issue was settled. Draupadi would be the common wife of the five brothers — a unique situation that would require clear rules. It was decided that she would live with each brother for a year in rotation, and that during that time, no other brother would intrude. If any brother did, he would have to go into exile for a year.

This pact, unusual as it was, was accepted by all.

Meanwhile, the news of the Pandavas being alive — and now married to Draupadi — reached Hastinapura. Duryodhana was stunned. The Pandavas had survived the fire. And now, they had won over the same Draupadi whom he and his brothers had failed to win at the swayamvara. His jealousy only grew deeper.

Bhishma, Drona, and even King Dhritarashtra were forced to acknowledge the Pandavas’ strength. It was decided that a peace agreement must be made, lest the kingdom fall into civil war. Thus, it was proposed that the Kuru kingdom be divided.
