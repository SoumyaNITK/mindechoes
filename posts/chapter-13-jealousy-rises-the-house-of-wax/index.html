Chapter 13 - Jealousy Rises: The House of Wax

2025-06-17

After the Kuru princes returned from their campaign against King Drupada, Drona was pleased. He had fulfilled his personal vow and proven the power of his students, especially Arjuna. Yet, as the fame of the Pandavas grew, so did the jealousy in Duryodhana's heart.

🔹 Arjuna’s Fame and Duryodhana’s Burning Jealousy

Arjuna was now the undisputed hero of the kingdom. His skill with the bow, his discipline, and his popularity with the people of Hastinapura made him the center of admiration. The citizens whispered praises, the elders nodded with pride, and even Bhishma smiled seeing Arjuna’s rise.

But for Duryodhana, each cheer for Arjuna was a slap across the face.

“How long,” he seethed, “before these Pandavas overshadow me completely?”

Duryodhana, with his cunning uncle Shakuni, began to hatch a plan. Not one of open confrontation—but of quiet elimination.

🔹 Dhritarashtra’s Dilemma and the New Plan

King Dhritarashtra, though blind, was not blind to the rising tension. He loved all his nephews, but his heart leaned toward his own son. Shakuni, ever the manipulator, took advantage of this and whispered dangerous ideas.

“My king,” he said, “why not send the Pandavas away on a diplomatic mission? Perhaps to a distant town. A show of trust, yes… and a test of fate.”

Dhritarashtra hesitated, but eventually agreed. A grand palace would be built for them in a town called Varnavata, far from the political heart of Hastinapura.

What the Pandavas didn’t know was—this palace would be made of highly flammable materials: lac, ghee, and wax.

A trap was being set.

🔹 Vidura’s Cryptic Warning

Before the Pandavas left, Vidura, ever watchful and wise, grew suspicious. He didn’t speak openly, but he approached Yudhishthira and whispered in the language of the forest tribes:

“He who knows the ways of mice can escape even a blazing house.”

Yudhishthira, puzzled at first, soon understood. Vidura was warning him in secret—something was not right in Varnavata.

🔹 The Journey and the Suspicion

The Pandavas, with their mother Kunti, traveled to Varnavata. The palace, called Lakshagriha (House of Lac), was beautiful—ornate and grand—but something felt off. The very walls smelled of oil and resin. The floors creaked with strange softness.

And so, under Vidura’s secret instructions, the Pandavas acted cautiously. They befriended a local miner and secretly had an underground tunnel dug from beneath the palace to the nearby forest.

They waited and watched.

🔹 The Sinister Plan Unfolds

One night, as expected, Purochana—the agent of Duryodhana—planned to set the palace ablaze while the Pandavas slept.

But that night, the Pandavas were not in their beds.

Instead, they set fire to the palace themselves. The flames engulfed the structure, and Purochana perished in the fire he had prepared for others.

The people of Varnavata woke to tragedy. They believed the Pandavas and Kunti had died in the inferno. Mourning spread through the land.

But in truth, the Pandavas had escaped through the hidden tunnel into the forest, beginning a new chapter of exile, survival, and destiny.
