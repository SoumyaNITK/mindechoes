Chapter 20 - The Division of the Kingdom

2025-06-24

After the Pandavas returned triumphantly with Draupadi, Hastinapura buzzed with surprise and curiosity. The citizens, who thought the Pandavas had perished in the fire, now rejoiced at their return. The elders in the royal court, especially Bhishma, Kripacharya, and Drona, were relieved and overjoyed to see the sons of Pandu alive. Even the blind king Dhritarashtra welcomed them warmly—though his heart carried mixed emotions.

A Royal Dilemma

When Dhritarashtra learned of Draupadi's marriage, he congratulated Yudhishthira and declared a grand celebration. But behind closed doors, his heart trembled. His son Duryodhana was far from pleased. The return of the Pandavas, especially with such a noble alliance, meant trouble for his ambitions. The thought that Yudhishthira might now stake claim to the throne filled Duryodhana with fury.

Duryodhana, along with Karna and his ever-cunning uncle Shakuni, went straight to Dhritarashtra and protested, "How can the sons of Pandu live in Hastinapura? This is our kingdom now! They should be sent away!"

The king, confused and conflicted, turned to the wise Bhishma, Vidura, and Drona. A meeting of the royal council was called.

Vidura’s Wisdom and a Compromise

Vidura, known for his fairness, rose and spoke calmly, “O King, injustice has already been done to the Pandavas. If we do not correct this now, we sow the seeds of future destruction. A kingdom divided in peace is better than a kingdom burned in civil war.”

The others nodded. Bhishma, Drona, and Kripacharya all supported Vidura’s view.

At last, Dhritarashtra agreed to a compromise: the kingdom would be divided. Yudhishthira and his brothers would be given a share of the kingdom and allowed to rule independently.

The Khandavaprastha Gift

Now came the real twist.

Duryodhana and Shakuni, with malice hidden behind fake smiles, suggested the Pandavas be given the barren, uninhabited forest land of Khandavaprastha—a desolate region filled with wild animals, thorny shrubs, and ruins of old settlements.

Thinking they were sending the Pandavas into hardship, Dhritarashtra agreed to give them Khandavaprastha. But what the Kauravas didn’t understand was this: the Pandavas had faced fire, exile, demons, and gods. A barren land was nothing in front of their courage and divine favor.

The Departure and a New Beginning

The Pandavas accepted the land with gratitude and humility. Yudhishthira said, “Let us make this place worthy of kingship. We will turn this forest into a heaven.”

So, accompanied by Draupadi, Kunti, and their allies, the five brothers left Hastinapura and journeyed to Khandavaprastha. With the help of Krishna and divine architects like Vishwakarma, they began to transform the jungle into a magnificent city.

Trees were cleared, rivers redirected, and roads constructed. Palaces rose, temples shimmered, and fountains danced. Birds returned, and flowers bloomed. What was once barren now became a paradise.

Thus was born the city of Indraprastha, named in honor of Indra, the king of gods.

It was not just a city—it was a symbol of hope, resilience, and divine grace.
