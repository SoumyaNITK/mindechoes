Chapter 14 - The Escape from the Burning Palace

2025-06-18

The flames roared behind them as the Pandavas, along with their mother Kunti, emerged from the secret tunnel into the thick cover of the forest. The night was eerily quiet, except for the distant crackle of the burning palace of wax. They had survived the trap. But survival meant sacrifice — they had to leave behind their names, their comforts, and even their identity.

In the eyes of the world, they were dead.

For days, they wandered deep into the woods, guided by Vidura’s secret messengers. Yudhishthira, calm and composed, kept his brothers focused. Bheema walked in front, clearing paths and carrying his mother when needed. Nakula and Sahadeva helped gather food and find shelter. Arjuna remained alert for threats, his hand never far from his bow.

The forest tested them — wild beasts, venomous snakes, and harsh weather became daily struggles. But it also brought them closer, united by hardship and purpose.

To avoid suspicion, they disguised themselves as wandering Brahmins, moving from one hermitage to another. In each village they visited, they listened to spiritual discourses, helped with chores, and lived off alms. They blended in, yet always remained alert, watching for any signs of Duryodhana’s spies.

Kunti, despite the sudden fall from royalty, never complained. Her strength became their anchor. One evening, as they sat under the open sky, she reminded them,
"A forest may be dark, but it cannot hide the light of dharma. Walk the path of righteousness, and the world will find you again."

Their life of exile had begun — but not with despair. It began with purpose.

And in the days ahead, fate would soon guide them to a turning point — one that would involve a beautiful princess, a mighty challenge, and a bow only one man could lift...
