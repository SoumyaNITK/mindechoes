Chapter 17 - The Demon Bakasura and Draupadi’s Swayamvara

2025-06-21

After the birth of Ghatotkacha, the Pandavas and Kunti continued living in hiding, disguised as Brahmins in the town of Ekachakra. Life was peaceful on the surface, but the town had a dark secret.

The Terror of Bakasura

Ekachakra was being tormented by a monstrous rakshasa named Bakasura, who lived in a nearby forest. Instead of rampaging through the town, he made a terrifying deal: the townsfolk must send him a daily cartload of food—along with the person who delivers it, to be devoured. Every household took turns sending someone, and the town lived in constant dread.

When it was finally the turn of the Brahmin family who had taken in the Pandavas, they broke down in despair. Hearing their sorrow, Bhima boldly offered to take their place. Kunti agreed, realizing that this was Bhima’s opportunity to use his immense strength for good.

Bhima set out, happily munching on the food meant for the demon. When Bakasura appeared, expecting a feast, he was furious to find Bhima eating it himself. A fierce battle followed, and despite Bakasura’s monstrous strength, Bhima overpowered him. After a brutal fight, Bhima killed the demon, freeing the town from fear forever.

The townsfolk were amazed, but Bhima and the Pandavas kept their identity secret. This heroic act added to their growing legend, even as they remained hidden.

News of Draupadi’s Swayamvara

Meanwhile, far away in the powerful kingdom of Panchala, ruled by King Drupada, exciting news spread across the land: a grand swayamvara was to be held for his daughter, Draupadi. Kings and warriors from across Bharat gathered to win her hand.

Drupada, still bitter over his humiliation by Drona, wanted the strongest and noblest warrior for his daughter—someone who could defeat Drona’s disciples. So, he devised a nearly impossible challenge: to string a massive bow and shoot an arrow through the eye of a rotating fish, by only looking at its reflection in water below.

The Pandavas, still in disguise, traveled to Panchala to witness the swayamvara. Draupadi was renowned for her beauty and grace, and many mighty kings—including Karna, the King of Anga—attended.

Karna boldly approached the bow, but before he could even try, Draupadi stopped him, saying, “I shall not marry a sutaputra (son of a charioteer).” The entire court was shocked. Humiliated, Karna stepped back, and the challenge remained unmet.

Finally, a Brahmin youth stepped forward—Arjuna in disguise. The crowd laughed at first, but as he picked up the enormous bow with ease, silence fell. Arjuna strung it effortlessly, took aim, and in a flash, the arrow pierced the eye of the fish perfectly.

The crowd erupted in disbelief and awe. Draupadi walked forward and placed the garland around Arjuna’s neck.

Escape and Return

The Kauravas and other rival kings were furious. A disguised Brahmin winning Draupadi? Unthinkable! A commotion broke out. But Bhima and Arjuna defended themselves and Draupadi, scattering the attackers. They returned to their humble dwelling with Draupadi, where Kunti, unaware of what had happened, said:

“Share whatever you’ve brought equally, as always, my sons.”

This statement would soon cause a great twist in their lives… one that would shape the destiny of the Pandavas forever.
